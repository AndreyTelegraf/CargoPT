# CargoPT Editorial Guide

Version: 1.0  
Locale: Portuguese (Portugal) — `pt-PT`  
Applies to: CargoPT guides, explanatory articles and long-form local content  
Primary objectives: answer the user, build trust, earn organic visibility and convert qualified demand into transport requests

## 1. Editorial purpose

CargoPT content is not a conventional blog.

Every guide must perform four jobs:

1. Answer a real question clearly and quickly.
2. Help the reader make a practical decision.
3. Demonstrate CargoPT's understanding of moving and transport in Portugal.
4. Lead naturally to a relevant next step, usually submitting one request to receive proposals.

The article must remain useful even when the reader does not submit a request.

Content must never withhold the answer merely to force a conversion.

## 2. Editorial promise

CargoPT provides practical, verifiable and Portugal-specific information about:

- moving costs;
- planning and preparation;
- furniture and appliance transport;
- packing and protection;
- access conditions, floors and lifts;
- choosing and checking transport providers;
- responsibility, insurance and damage;
- moving services in Portuguese cities and municipalities.

The reader should leave each page knowing:

- what matters;
- what to do next;
- what information to provide;
- what uncertainties remain;
- when professional transport may be necessary.

## 3. Voice and tone

The default voice is:

- direct;
- calm;
- competent;
- practical;
- neutral;
- specific to Portugal;
- respectful of the reader's time.

Use European Portuguese.

Prefer ordinary Portuguese terms understood by clients. Introduce technical terminology only when it improves accuracy, and explain it immediately.

Address the reader as `você` only when necessary. Prefer neutral constructions such as:

- `Indique...`
- `Confirme...`
- `Antes da mudança...`
- `O preço depende de...`

Do not imitate named authors, publications or competitors.

Do not use:

- literary digressions;
- inflated claims;
- artificial urgency;
- vague motivational language;
- excessive adjectives;
- jokes that weaken trust;
- generic AI-style introductions;
- phrases such as `neste artigo vamos explorar`;
- repeated references to CargoPT in every section.

## 4. Accuracy standard

Factual accuracy takes priority over style and keyword coverage.

Never invent:

- prices;
- legal obligations;
- insurance coverage;
- average durations;
- market statistics;
- technical limits;
- carrier availability;
- guarantees;
- customer reviews;
- completed-job data.

A statement must be treated as one of the following:

### 4.1 Stable practical fact

Examples:

- stairs and floors affect handling effort;
- larger volumes usually require more capacity;
- insufficient descriptions make proposals harder to compare.

These may be stated directly when they are operationally clear and not dependent on changing law or market data.

### 4.2 Variable market information

Examples:

- typical prices;
- hourly rates;
- seasonal demand;
- waiting times;
- regional availability.

These require a dated and attributable source, CargoPT's own verified dataset, or explicit qualification.

Use wording such as:

- `O valor varia conforme...`
- `Não existe um preço único...`
- `Para comparar valores, descreva o mesmo serviço a todos os transportadores.`

Do not present an unsupported numerical range merely because it appears plausible.

### 4.3 Legal or safety-sensitive information

Legal, insurance, tax, licensing and safety claims require authoritative current sources.

The article must:

- identify the jurisdiction;
- state the verification date;
- distinguish legal requirements from practical recommendations;
- avoid presenting general information as individual legal advice.

When reliable confirmation is unavailable, say so plainly.

## 5. Search intent

Each article must serve one primary query and one dominant intent.

Allowed intent classes:

- informational;
- commercial;
- transactional;
- local.

The primary query must appear naturally in:

- the page title;
- the H1;
- the opening answer;
- at least one section heading when appropriate;
- metadata;
- structured data where applicable.

Do not repeat the exact keyword mechanically.

Use semantic variants only where they improve the answer.

## 6. Standard article structure

Every full guide should normally follow this order.

### 6.1 Breadcrumb

Show the reader where the article belongs:

`CargoPT → Guias → Cluster → Article`

The visible breadcrumb and `BreadcrumbList` structured data must agree.

### 6.2 Hero

Include:

- editorial category;
- one H1;
- a short description;
- publication or review date when the subject may change;
- a concise CTA relevant to the question.

The hero must not occupy so much space that it hides the answer on mobile.

### 6.3 Direct answer

The first substantive block must answer the query immediately.

Target length: 60–140 words.

For a price question, explain first that there is no single fixed price and list the main variables before expanding.

For a procedural question, give the essential sequence first.

For a local page, identify the service area and the main local constraints first.

### 6.4 Key points

Provide a compact summary with three to six decision-useful points.

The summary must work independently for:

- users scanning the page;
- search-result extraction;
- AI assistants;
- social reuse.

### 6.5 Main explanation

Divide the answer into clear H2 sections.

Each section must answer a distinct sub-question.

Use H3 only when a section contains materially different subsections.

Avoid sections created solely to place another keyword.

### 6.6 Practical examples

Use examples to explain variables and decisions.

Examples must be labelled as illustrative unless they come from verified CargoPT data.

Never present an illustrative scenario as a market quotation.

### 6.7 Action checklist

Include concrete information the reader should collect or actions they should take.

For transport requests, this often includes:

- exact origin and destination;
- object list;
- dimensions where relevant;
- estimated volume;
- floors;
- lift availability and dimensions;
- parking or loading restrictions;
- required helpers;
- desired date and flexibility;
- dismantling or packing needs;
- photographs when useful.

### 6.8 FAQ

Include three to six genuine follow-up questions.

FAQ answers must add information rather than repeat entire sections.

Visible FAQ content and `FAQPage` structured data must match exactly when structured data is used.

### 6.9 Related content

Link only to pages that materially help the reader continue the task.

Prefer:

- one parent or cluster page;
- two to four closely related guides;
- one relevant transactional landing page.

Do not create circular link blocks with no editorial purpose.

### 6.10 Final CTA

The final CTA must connect the article's subject to CargoPT's service.

Preferred value proposition:

- describe the transport once;
- provide the same details to suitable carriers;
- receive proposals;
- compare before deciding.

Do not promise a specific number of proposals.

Do not promise the lowest price.

## 7. Page hierarchy

Each page must contain:

- exactly one H1;
- logical H2 sections;
- H3 only below an H2;
- no skipped heading levels;
- no heading used only for visual styling.

Recommended H1 length: concise enough to display clearly on mobile.

Do not force every title into the same formula when a clearer natural title exists.

## 8. Paragraphs and readability

Prefer short paragraphs.

Typical paragraph length: one to four sentences.

Use lists when they make comparison or action easier.

Do not convert normal prose into excessive bullet lists.

Each paragraph should make one main point.

Avoid:

- long preambles;
- repeated conclusions;
- several consecutive paragraphs saying the same thing;
- filler inserted to reach a word count.

Article length follows the question, not a fixed SEO target.

A complete 900-word guide is better than a repetitive 2,000-word guide.

## 9. Price content

Price articles require special discipline.

A price guide must explain:

- why there is no universal price;
- the variables that materially affect the quote;
- what information is needed for a meaningful proposal;
- why apparently similar moves may cost differently;
- how to compare proposals on equivalent scope;
- which services may be included or excluded.

Potential variables include:

- route and distance;
- quantity and volume;
- object weight and handling complexity;
- number of workers;
- floors and stairs;
- lift access;
- walking distance between vehicle and property;
- parking and loading restrictions;
- dismantling and assembly;
- packing materials and packing labour;
- special equipment;
- date, time and flexibility;
- return route or combined transport opportunities.

Do not publish price tables until their methodology and source date can be shown.

When CargoPT later has sufficient verified data, price examples must state:

- sample period;
- geographic scope;
- sample size;
- included and excluded services;
- whether values are proposals or completed-job prices;
- median or distribution rather than an unsupported simple average.

## 10. City and local guides

A city guide must contain real local utility rather than swapping a city name in a template.

Relevant local elements may include:

- dense or historic areas;
- narrow streets;
- loading and parking constraints;
- apartment access patterns;
- nearby municipalities commonly served;
- local-to-local and intercity routes;
- information the client should provide before requesting proposals.

Do not claim municipal rules without checking the responsible authority.

Do not manufacture neighbourhood expertise.

Local pages must be sufficiently distinct to deserve separate indexing.

## 11. Object-specific guides

Object guides should address:

- preparation;
- dimensions and weight;
- disconnection or emptying where applicable;
- safe handling position;
- protection;
- route through doors, stairs and lifts;
- number of handlers;
- special equipment;
- risks;
- information and photographs required for a quote.

Do not give unsafe technical instructions.

For manufacturer-specific requirements, recommend checking the manufacturer's documentation.

## 12. Legal, responsibility and insurance guides

These pages require:

- authoritative sources;
- verification date;
- careful terminology;
- explicit separation of law, contract terms and recommended practice;
- a notice that the article provides general information.

Never state that all carriers have the same insurance or liability conditions.

Recommend obtaining relevant conditions in writing before the service.

## 13. CargoPT positioning

Describe CargoPT accurately.

Approved positioning:

- CargoPT is a request and comparison service for moving and transport in Portugal.
- The client describes the service once.
- CargoPT can route the request to suitable independent carriers.
- Carriers may send their own proposals.
- The client compares the available proposals and decides.

Do not describe CargoPT as:

- the transport provider;
- the party physically performing the move;
- an insurer;
- a guarantor of every carrier;
- the cheapest option;
- a service that guarantees multiple proposals.

Do not use `Uber das mudanças` as formal editorial positioning.

It may help explain the concept informally, but it weakens precision and brand ownership.

## 14. Conversion rules

Editorial content must support conversion without becoming an advertisement.

Use CTAs:

- after the direct answer when commercially relevant;
- after a decision or checklist section;
- at the end of the article.

Maximum recommended prominent CTA count: three.

CTA text should describe the next action.

Preferred examples:

- `Receber propostas`
- `Descrever a mudança`
- `Pedir propostas para este transporte`

Avoid:

- `Comprar agora`
- `Não perca`
- `Última oportunidade`
- `Garantir o melhor preço`

## 15. Internal linking

Every published guide must link to:

- `/guias/`;
- at least two relevant published pages when available;
- the main request form or a relevant commercial landing page.

Anchor text must describe the destination naturally.

Avoid repeated exact-match keyword anchors across every page.

Do not link to planned pages that return an empty or generic response.

A registry relationship does not automatically mean a visible link should be published before the target page exists.

## 16. Metadata

Every article must define:

- unique `<title>`;
- unique meta description;
- canonical URL;
- `pt-PT` and `x-default` alternate links;
- Open Graph title;
- Open Graph description;
- Open Graph URL;
- Open Graph image;
- Twitter card metadata.

The title and description must accurately represent visible content.

Do not include unsupported superlatives.

## 17. Structured data

Use only structured data that matches the visible page.

Typical guide types:

- `Article`;
- `BreadcrumbList`;
- `FAQPage` when a visible FAQ exists.

An `Article` should normally include:

- headline;
- description;
- mainEntityOfPage;
- author or publisher;
- datePublished;
- dateModified;
- image;
- articleSection;
- inLanguage.

Do not add rating, review, price or offer schema without real matching data.

Structured data must be valid JSON.

## 18. Dates and maintenance

Every guide must have:

- publication date;
- modification date;
- review responsibility.

Update `dateModified` only when the substantive content changes.

Time-sensitive guides should include a visible review date.

Review frequency:

- legal and regulatory pages: whenever the source changes and at least every six months;
- price and market pages: at least every three months once numerical data is published;
- practical evergreen guides: at least annually;
- city pages: when local operational information changes.

## 19. Sources and citations

Use primary and authoritative Portuguese sources whenever the statement requires verification.

Preferred source hierarchy:

1. Portuguese legislation and official government portals.
2. Municipal and regulatory authorities.
3. Manufacturer documentation for object handling.
4. CargoPT's verified internal dataset.
5. Established industry or consumer sources with transparent methodology.

Do not cite a source merely because it ranks highly in search.

Do not copy competitor wording or structures.

Quotes should be rare and short. Prefer accurate paraphrase with attribution.

## 20. AI and answer-engine readiness

The article must be easy to extract without being written for machines at the expense of humans.

Use:

- direct definitions;
- concise answer blocks;
- explicit conditions;
- meaningful headings;
- consistent terminology;
- clear lists;
- visible source dates;
- unambiguous internal links.

Avoid:

- hidden content written only for crawlers;
- keyword inventories;
- repeated near-identical answers;
- claims that CargoPT is the only or first service unless independently verified and currently supportable.

## 21. Originality and duplication

Every article must provide a distinct answer.

Do not create pages by replacing only:

- city;
- object;
- route;
- keyword.

Before publication, compare the new page against related content.

A page should not be published when it adds no meaningful information beyond an existing page.

Shared CargoPT explanations may be concise and reused conceptually, but the surrounding article must remain specific to its query.

## 22. Accessibility

Content must remain understandable without visual decoration.

Requirements:

- descriptive link text;
- logical heading structure;
- no information conveyed only through colour;
- tables must have headings;
- images require useful alternative text unless decorative;
- controls require accessible labels;
- mobile reading must not require horizontal scrolling.

## 23. Prohibited claims

Do not publish claims such as:

- `o mais barato`;
- `o melhor transportador`;
- `preço garantido`;
- `resposta garantida`;
- `transportadores disponíveis em todas as zonas`;
- `todos os transportadores são verificados`;
- `sem qualquer risco`;
- `primeiro agregador de Portugal`;

unless the exact claim has current evidence, approved wording and a documented verification basis.

## 24. Pre-publication checklist

A guide is ready only when all applicable checks pass.

### Editorial

- The page answers the primary query in the opening block.
- The text uses European Portuguese.
- Every factual claim is supportable.
- Variable information is qualified or sourced.
- No invented price or statistic appears.
- The article contains practical next steps.
- The CTA does not overpromise.
- CargoPT's role is described accurately.
- The article is distinct from existing pages.

### Structure

- Exactly one H1.
- Heading levels are logical.
- The page has a direct answer.
- Key points are concise.
- FAQ adds useful follow-up information.
- Internal links point only to valid public pages.
- The final CTA is relevant.

### Technical SEO

- Unique title and meta description.
- Canonical matches the public URL.
- `hreflang` is correct.
- Open Graph metadata is complete.
- Structured data parses successfully.
- Visible FAQ and FAQ schema agree.
- The URL appears once in the sitemap.
- The registry status is `published`.
- `datePublished` and `dateModified` are valid.

### Quality assurance

- Static smoke test passes.
- Existing guide, landing and registry smoke tests pass.
- `git diff --check` passes.
- Mobile and desktop rendering are inspected.
- The public URL and sitemap are verified after publication.

## 25. First-guide implementation standard

The first reference article is:

`Quanto custa uma mudança em Portugal?`

Its purpose is to establish the reusable article architecture.

Until reliable numerical market data is available, it must not invent price ranges.

It should answer:

- why there is no single fixed price;
- which variables affect the proposal;
- what is normally included or may be charged separately;
- how to request comparable proposals;
- how to reduce avoidable cost;
- what information to provide;
- how CargoPT fits into the comparison process.

Once this reference page is approved, its semantic structure may be reused for other guides, but its content must not become a generic template copied without query-specific adaptation.
