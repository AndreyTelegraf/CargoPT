Damage during service

Who is liable for damage during a move?

The answer depends on what happened, the agreement, the evidence and the conditions applicable to the service.

Published on 2026-07-24 · Reviewed by CargoPT

Direct answer

Liability depends on the case and must be documented

It is not possible to automatically determine who pays for a damage without analyzing how it occurred, who had the item under control, the agreed service and the applicable conditions. Record the condition of the goods before the move, report the damage as soon as it is detected and keep photographs, messages, quote and proof. Ask the carrier for a written response and check possible coverage before accepting a solution.

Key points

What to do in the event of damage

  • Photograph valuable and fragile possessions before service.
  • Record the damage as soon as it is detected.
  • Save quote, messages and proof.
  • Ask the carrier for a written response.
  • Check conditions, limits and possible coverage.

Document the condition of belongings before moving

Dated photographs help distinguish past damage from occurrences during service.

Especially note fragile furniture, corners, appliances, glass, works and valuable items.

  • Photograph multiple faces of the item.
  • Register existing risks and defects.
  • Save serial numbers when relevant.
  • Confirm transported inventory.
  • Photograph the packaging of fragile items.
  • Save the description sent to the carrier.

What to do when you detect damage

Do not alter or immediately discard the packaging or parts related to the damage.

Photograph the item, packaging and location, record the date and communicate the problem in writing.

Compare the damage with the agreed service

Check who was responsible for packaging, disassembly, loading, transportation, unloading and assembly.

The analysis may be different when the item was packaged by the customer or when there were unreported defects or weaknesses.

Gather relevant information

Organize the quote, messages, photographs, proof and chronological description.

Avoid vague statements. Identify the item, the damage observed, when it was detected and the solution requested.

  • Service quote or agreement.
  • Inventory shipped.
  • Before and after photographs.
  • Messages exchanged.
  • Proof of payment.
  • Repair quote when available.

Check if there is applicable coverage

Ask the carrier if there is coverage for the goods and ask for information about risks, limits, exclusions and procedures.

Compulsory car insurance should not automatically be confused with furniture coverage.

Covered risk

Confirm which incident is covered.

Limit

Check the applicable maximum value.

Exclusions

Ask about non-covered assets or circumstances.

Participation

Confirm documents and deadlines indicated for the case.

Ask for a written response and solution

Present the problem itemively and ask for a written response within a reasonable time.

When there is no agreement, seek information from a consumer support entity or legal advice appropriate to the case.

Register the service before moving

A full description and written quotes make it clearer what each party has agreed to do.

Frequently asked questions

Questions about damage during moves

Does the carrier always pay for any damage?

It is not possible to say this without analyzing the cause, the agreement, the evidence and the applicable conditions.

Should I photograph the furniture before moving?

Yes, especially fragile, valuable or previously damaged items.

Can I report damage after delivery?

Report it as soon as it is detected and keep proof of the item's condition and date.

Does car insurance cover furniture?

Don't assume so. Confirm specifically what coverage exists for the goods being transported.

What to keep when filing a complaint?

Keep the quote, messages, photographs, inventory, proof and any repair quote.
