Preparing the refrigerator

How to prepare a refrigerator for transport

Preparing in advance reduces the risk of water, odors, loose parts and damage when moving the refrigerator.

Published on 2026-07-24 · Reviewed by CargoPT

Direct answer

Prepare the refrigerator before the carrier arrives

Before transporting, empty the refrigerator, unplug it early enough to defrost, remove the water and clean the interior. Remove or secure shelves, drawers and other moving parts. Protect the cable, keep doors closed without applying tape directly to delicate surfaces and inform the carrier about dimensions, access and stairs.

Key points

Essential steps

  • Remove food, containers and loose items.
  • Unplug and defrost equipment before collection.
  • Completely dry the interior and surrounding floor.
  • Remove or secure shelves, drawers and cables.
  • Confirm dimensions, access and transport position.

Empty the fridge and plan your meals

Remove all food, bottles, containers and accessories before transport. The equipment must remain empty.

Plan the consumption, donation or temporary storage of refrigerated and frozen products. A portable refrigerator or cooler can be useful during the move.

  • Remove food from the refrigerated and freezer areas.
  • Empty ice bins and water dispensers.
  • Remove magnets and items placed outside.
  • Separate products that require temporary refrigeration.

Shut down and defrost in advance

The time required depends on the model, amount of ice and manufacturer's instructions. Consult the equipment manual and do not leave this task until collection time.

Place suitable containers or towels to collect the water. Do not use sharp items or force ice, as this may damage internal components.

Clean and dry the interior

After defrosting, clean the internal surfaces and remove all moisture. Residual water may spill during movement and cause odors if the equipment remains closed.

Let the inside air out while you finish preparing. Before collection, confirm that the floor and the bottom of the appliance are dry.

Protect shelves, drawers and cables

Glass shelves, drawers, supports and other components may shift during transport. Remove the most fragile parts and pack them separately or secure them according to the manufacturer's instructions.

Secure the electrical cable to the equipment body without bending it excessively. Water supply pipes, when present, must be disconnected and drained correctly.

Glass shelves

Remove and protect them individually when they cannot be securely fixed.

Drawers and supports

Remove or immobilize components that could open or knock.

Electrical cable

Roll it up loosely and secure it to prevent it from getting stuck under the appliance.

Water connection

Shut off the power, disconnect the pipe and dispose of residual water when applicable.

Close doors and protect surfaces

Doors must remain closed during movement. Use straps or appropriate protection, avoiding materials that leave residue or damage the finish.

Shipping blankets help protect doors, corners and panels. Do not block handles or points necessary to move the equipment.

What information to send to the carrier

Indicate the model or approximate dimensions of the refrigerator, the weight when known and whether it is American, combined or large equipment.

Describe the floors, elevators, stairs, corridors, doors and parking in both locations. Send photos when space is tight or there are obstacles.

  • Approximate height, width and depth.
  • Refrigerator type and configuration.
  • Floors and existence of an elevator.
  • Width of doors, corridors and stairs.
  • Distance between the entrance and the vehicle.
  • Need for helpers or additional equipment.

Frequently asked questions

Questions about preparing the refrigerator

How long before should I unplug the refrigerator?

It depends on the model and the amount of ice. Consult the manufacturer's manual and allow enough time to thaw, clean and dry completely.

Should shelves be removed?

Remove fragile parts or those that cannot be safely immobilized. Pack glass shelves separately.

Can I transport food inside the refrigerator?

It is not recommended. Equipment must be emptied to avoid spills, additional weight and internal damage.

Should I tape the doors shut?

Doors should be secure, but directly applied tape can leave residue or damage finishes. Choose straps and adequate protection.

What photos should I send?

Photograph the refrigerator, doors, corridors, stairs, elevators and any obstacle between the equipment and the place where the vehicle can stop.
